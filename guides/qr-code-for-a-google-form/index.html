How to Make a QR Code for a Google Form

By Bud Hennekes, who built QR Codes Made Easy. Updated August 2026.

Google Forms doesn't build its own QR code. Click Send, copy the link (shorten it if you like), turn off any setting that forces a sign-in you didn't intend, then paste that link into a QR code generator. The result opens straight into your form on any phone camera, no app required.

Why Forms doesn't have a "QR code" button

People assume the Send dialog has a built-in QR option next to email and link sharing. It doesn't. Google Forms gives you a link (with an optional shortener) and an embed snippet, not a QR code. To get a QR code, you copy that link and run it through a separate generator, the same way you'd make a code for any other web page.

That's actually the cleaner path. A link-based QR code is static: it always opens the form at that address, and you're not depending on a Forms feature that could change. If you'd rather skip a step next time, our generator keeps that link-to-code conversion to under a minute.

Get the right form link

  1. Open your form in edit mode and click the purple Send button.
  2. Choose the link icon (it looks like two chain links).
  3. Tick "Shorten URL" if you want a tidier link to type as a backup.
  4. Copy the link and paste it into a private or incognito browser tab to confirm it opens the live form, not the editor.

If you want people to land on a form with certain fields already filled in, for example an event name or table number, use Forms' Get pre-filled link option from the three-dot menu instead of the plain Send link: fill the form out once with the values you want locked in, generate the link, and encode that instead. It saves the respondent a step and cuts down on typos in fields you already know the answer to.

Check the sign-in wall before you print

The most common reason a Google Form QR code frustrates people is a setting that forces them to sign in with a Google account before they can answer. Google's own help documentation confirms that turning on Limit to 1 response means "users must sign in to their Google Account" to fill it out, and that a form can separately be restricted "to a domain, trusted audience, or group of users" before it's published.

Do this before you print: open Settings → Responses in your form. If "Limit to 1 response" or an organization restriction is on and you want a stranger, guest, or non-work email to be able to answer from a scan, turn it off. Then test the link signed out of every Google account, the same way you'd test any public page.

Make the code

Open QR Codes Made Easy, leave Website selected, and paste the form link you tested. Then:

  1. Start with dark on light. It's the fastest combination for a phone camera to lock onto, especially in a busy room.
  2. Keep the link short. The plain Send link is shorter and produces a cleaner code than a heavily parameterized pre-filled link; use the pre-filled version only when the convenience is worth a slightly denser pattern.
  3. Download SVG for print, PNG for screens. SVG stays sharp on a poster or table sign; PNG is easiest to drop into a slide or email. Both are generated in your browser, and your link never passes through a server in between.

The 60-second print-proof protocol

Before a real print run, place the final code in its actual layout and print one proof at final size. Scan it with two phones, once in good light and once in ordinary room light, and confirm three things: it scans without hunting, it opens the form (not a "request access" screen), and the form actually accepts a test response. Submit that test response, then check it landed in the results, and delete it afterward.

For sizing, our QR print-size guide covers the rule of thumb for signs, table tents, and handouts. If a proof won't scan, work through the troubleshooting checklist before you print a full batch.

Where a Google Form QR code actually earns its spot

  • Event check-in or RSVP: pair it with a pre-filled link so the event name is already set.
  • Feedback or survey card: print it on a receipt, table tent, or exit sign with a one-line reason to scan.
  • Sign-up sheet replacement: a class list, volunteer sheet, or waitlist that used to be a clipboard.
  • Job or vendor application: a flyer or bulletin-board posting that sends applicants straight to the intake form.

Say what the scan gets someone before they scan it. "Leave feedback (2 minutes)" outperforms an unlabeled square every time, since people commit to short, clearly-scoped forms far more readily than mystery links.

FAQ

Does Google Forms have a built-in QR code option?

No. Forms' Send dialog offers a link and an embed snippet, not a QR code. You copy the form link and generate the QR code with a separate tool.

Why does my form ask people to sign in before answering?

Usually one of two settings: "Limit to 1 response," which Google's help documentation confirms requires a signed-in Google account, or an organization restriction limiting responses to your domain. Turn off whichever one doesn't fit your audience.

Can I pre-fill part of the form from the QR code?

Yes. Use Forms' "Get pre-filled link" option to lock in known answers, like an event name, then generate your QR code from that link instead of the plain Send link.

Will the QR code still work if I edit the form later?

Yes, as long as the form itself isn't deleted. The code encodes the form's URL, and editing questions, adding fields, or changing the title doesn't change that address.
